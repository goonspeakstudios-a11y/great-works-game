# The Republic: the city in speech (design for the next sprint)

Decided with BB1 (2026-09-28): the Republic's mini-game is "logical, almost semi-CK3 political
planning": a city you found and govern, whose people have hidden natures, where every law has
consequences you can read, and which turns out, at the end, to have been your own soul. Nothing
here is built yet except the board (`Thinkers.city_in_speech`, round 2), which already has the
stages, the slots for what comes next, the three orders and the soul.

## The frame
- You are the founder: "we are founders of a city" (379a). Socrates walks beside you on the hillside
  above the board; Glaucon and Adeimantus push back, as in the book.
- The board grows by stages (the city of pigs, the fevered city, the army, the guardians, the just
  city). Each stage is reached by decisions, not by time, and is one lesson point.
- Zen: the stages can be walked through, reading Socrates' reasons. Student: the teacher must hear
  why, in your own words, before a stage counts.

## Characters (the CK3 part)
- Every citizen is a person with a name, a portrait (People.figure), and a hidden **metal** (gold,
  silver, bronze or iron: the myth of the metals, 415a) plus a few **traits**: love of learning,
  spirit, appetite, memory, courage, gentleness, greed.
- You do not see a metal at first. **Trials** reveal it: tests of memory and of pleasure and fear,
  "as gold is tried in the fire" (413d-e). A trial costs a season.
- Children do not always share their parents' metal: a gold child of bronze parents must be raised
  up, a bronze child of gold parents set down (415b-c). Getting this wrong is the city's slow poison.

## Offices and orders
- Three orders: **producers** (farmers, builders, weavers, cobblers, smiths, herdsmen, merchants),
  **auxiliaries** (the soldiers), **rulers** (the guardians proper).
- Each person does one work (370b). A person in the wrong order shows it: a bronze soul among the
  rulers grows greedy, a silver one kept at the plough grows restless.

## Resources and meters
- **Food** and **land** (the city of pigs has enough; the fevered city never does).
- **Luxury**: couches, perfumes, painters, cooks, doctors. It pleases, and it needs more land.
- **Wealth and poverty**: both corrupt the craftsman (421d-422a); the rulers own nothing.
- **Unity**: "one city, not two", the rich and the poor at war inside it (422e-423a).
- **Justice**: each order doing its own work and not meddling in the others' (433a-434c). The
  measure the game is really about.

## Edicts (laws you pass, each with its reasons and its costs)
- One man, one work.
- Admit luxuries, or keep the city of pigs.
- War for the neighbours' land (the origin of war, 373d-e).
- The curriculum: music and gymnastics; which stories of the gods may be told (the poets censored,
  the gods shown only as good, 377-383); the poet who can imitate anything, anointed with myrrh,
  crowned with wool and sent to another city (398a). Homer's own place is the book before: this is
  where the game turns on its own last level.
- The noble lie of the metals.
- No private property, gold or houses for the guardians (416d-417b).
- Women guardians trained and ruling with the men (451c-457b).
- The marriage festivals by rigged lot, and children raised in common (458c-461e).
- Philosopher-kings (473c-e): the last and hardest law.

## Events
- A gold child born to bronze parents, or the reverse.
- Luxury creeping in: a new trade asks to be admitted.
- A poet at the gate.
- The rich and the poor becoming two cities.
- The neighbours' war.
- A guardian caught with gold.

## The end: the city turns round into the soul
- When each order does its own work, the just city is complete, and the board turns round into the
  soul over it (already built: bronze belly, silver chest, gold head, 434d-441c).
- Then generations pass. The marriage number is missed (546a-d), the wrong metals rise, and the city
  declines regime by regime, each shown on the board with its matching soul: timocracy (honour),
  oligarchy (wealth), democracy (freedom), tyranny (the worst of appetite) (Book VIII-IX).
- No ending is a win; the lesson is why each regime fails, and what that says about a soul.

## Lesson points (six, as each place has)
1. One man, one work (the city of pigs).
2. Why cities go to war (the fevered city).
3. The noble lie and the three orders.
4. Justice in the city: each doing their own.
5. The three parts of the soul (the city turned round).
6. The decline of regimes, and the tyrant's soul.

## Where it lives
The Academy (Plato's place, zone `academy`), with the other Plato areas from round 2 round it: the
court of the Apology, Cephalus's house, Gyges' chasm, the divided line, the cave, Er's meadow,
Agathon's dinner, the prison.
