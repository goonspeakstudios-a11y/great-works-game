# Sprint 4: the next three rooms, and playing Homer as its heroes

**Goal:** the next three books on the reading list get their places, built to the Sprint 3 standard
(a teacher who answers from the real text, a lesson stone with a scene for every point, the place
lost to the world and mended piece by piece), and the Iliad and Odyssey are *played*: you stand
where the heroes stood, as them, with their arms and what they hold in view.

Decided with BB1 (2026-09-28): reading order (Rhetorica, Euclid, Homer; the Republic next sprint);
Homer's moments are guided, not action or choice: one short line at a time, one physical act, no
failing; the Republic is to be "logical, almost semi-CK3 political planning" (see
`docs/republic-city.md`). The set pieces all come from the round 2 kits (`docs/assets.md`).

## Stories
1. **Beats** (`scripts/hall/beat.gd`, `content/greece/beats.json`): a moment of a book played in the
   first person. Steps: a line, an act (press or hold F), a walk to a mark (a glow shows where),
   the view turning, someone moving, something appearing, your eyes dropping (kneeling), what a
   hand holds changing. As a hero, his forearms and what he holds hang before your eyes. Esc leaves
   it; it can be replayed; everything it moved is put back. When it ends its point is read like a
   stone's: it counts for a Theoros, not for a Mathetes.
2. **#4 Rhetorica ad Herennium, a house in Rome, about 85 BC:** the Author in his tablinum, the
   atrium, the busts of Demosthenes and Crassus, a new map of 85 BC; a memory house along the street
   (twenty places, the first four with their images); a court in the Forum. Six stones: the five
   tasks, the six parts of a speech, the three styles, places, images, and the **memory walk**:
   set five strange images on five places, go to court, walk the places again and speak. The
   Author answers from the Latin (no English is out of copyright) and says plainly what it means.
3. **#5 Euclid, the Museum at Alexandria, about 300 BC:** Euclid at his sand tray in the Museum's
   court, the Pharos far off. Stones for the definitions, the postulates, the common notions and the
   bridge of asses; **rope and pegs** (peg A and B, walk the two circles, find C) for Proposition 1;
   **count the tiles** (9 + 16 = 25) for Proposition 47. Heath's translation of Book I.
4. **#6 Homer, the plain of Troy and the way home:** Homer by his fire between the ships and the city;
   the camp, Achilles' hut, Hephaestus's forge and the shield, the walls and the Scaean gate; a black
   ship to the Cyclops' island, past the Sirens, to the edge of Ocean, and home to Ithaca. Eight
   points, each a beat as its hero: Achilles' sword and Athena's hand (Il. 1), Hector's helmet (6),
   the embassy refused (9), walking the shield (18), the chase round the walls (22), Priam kneeling
   (24), "Noman" and the ram (Od. 9), the bow and the bed (21-23). Also played, not points: the
   Sirens, and the dead at the trench, who drift in toward the blood while you are near and shrink
   back while you hold the sword out. Butler's translation; every quoted line is his.
5. Each place has its music (rome, alexandria, troy, from round 2), each piece brought back changing
   what you hear; its facts along the way in; its map; its book on a stand.

## Playtest checklist for BB1
- The Shore: are the gateways to #4, #5 and #6 open, and the rest still sealed?
- Rhetorica: does the house read as Roman? Walk the memory walk: is it clear where to go, and does
  walking your places again in court feel like remembering? Do the Author's Latin quotations help
  or get in the way?
- Euclid: peg out Proposition 1. Does walking the circles make the proof feel true? Count the tiles.
- Homer: play every beat. Do you feel like Achilles, Hector, Priam, Odysseus? Are the arms and what
  they hold right, and not in the way? Is one short line at a time the right pace?
- The voyage: is the ship easy to take, and are the islands easy to find again?
- The edge of Ocean: is it frightening? Too much, or not enough?
- Lost to whole in each place: does each piece that comes back feel earned, and can you hear it?
- Anything annoying, confusing or ugly?
