# Sprint 2: Aristotle's Lyceum

**Goal:** the second place on the reading list, built to the same standard as the villa: it looks
and feels like Athens on a bright morning in 335 BC, Aristotle is there in person, and the
Categories are taught in his own words from the book itself.

## Stories
1. The place: the path up from the gateway between plane trees and the Ilissos, the covered
   walkway (stoa) with the facts on its wall, the busts of Plato and Alexander, the map of 335 BC
   and the book; the Acropolis and the city far off; a bench by the stream.
2. Aristotle walks: he paces the walkway as he teaches (the Peripatetic), stops and turns to you
   when you come near, and you talk to him wherever he is.
3. His own music: pan pipes over a walking bass, birdsong, the stream, a little wind.
4. The lesson: six points from Categories 1-5 (the ten kinds, homonyms, said-of and present-in,
   primary substance, secondary substance, the marks of substance), judged in your own words.
5. Areas that wait for later lessons: the library (for the rest of the Organon) and the sacred
   grove across the footbridge (for the Ethics), both closed for now.
6. (BB1) Alexander's men: a block of sixteen pikemen marching a circuit of the meadow beyond the
   grove, the sixteen-rayed sun of Macedon on every shield; F tells you who they are.
7. The Categories text (Edghill, public domain) in the library of passages, 63 of them.

## Playtest checklist for BB1
- Does the Lyceum feel like a different place from the villa (light, sound, pace)?
- Did you find Aristotle without being told where? Did you like walking beside him?
- Ask him the four suggested questions and one of your own. Do his answers sound like him?
- Watch the soldiers for a minute. Do they read as Macedonians?
- Anything annoying, confusing or ugly?
