## Pacing and time gates (2026-09-26)
- Force a pace so players cannot rush through: time gates that hold back the next part until
  some time has passed, so the material is appreciated even in Zen mode.
- Open: what the gate is (real time between lessons? time spent in a place? a daily limit?),
  whether Student and Zen gate differently, and how a gate is shown without promising
  anything (see the no-hope tone rule).

## East Asia: two gates, China and Japan (2026-09-26)
- Near-critical that there be two: a Chinese gate and a Japanese gate, not one "Asia" door.
  Today worlds.json has one world "asia" (India, China and Japan) with a torii door; this would
  split it (a Chinese paifang for China, the torii for Japan; India's place still to decide).
- Can be changed later.

## The Mishima world (2026-09-26)
- Shaped much like Paul Schrader's film "Mishima: A Life in Four Chapters" (1985): Beauty
  (The Temple of the Golden Pavilion), Art (Kyoko's House), Action (Runaway Horses), and the
  Harmony of Pen and Sword (his last day, 25 November 1970).
- Plus "Sun and Steel" (1968), his late essay on body, word and action, among his last works
  (the Sea of Fertility tetralogy was finished on the day he died).

## Zen / Student choice per world (2026-09-26, being grilled)
- Asked on first entering each world, in that world's own words. Greece: Theoros (the pilgrim
  who comes to see) for Zen, Mathetes (the pupil) for Student; Akroates (hearer of the closed
  lectures) is the alternative.
- Worlds with several cultures and languages: decide when we get there.
